# AI governance policy

## Purpose

This policy sets how {{organization}} develops, deploys and uses AI systems and agents: who is accountable for them, what they may do, how they are tested and watched, and what people are told about them.

## Scope

It covers every AI system the organization builds or operates, every AI agent that acts on its behalf (including agents that develop its software), and the third-party models and services they rely on. It applies alongside the information security, privacy and acceptable use policies; where they differ, the stricter rule applies.

## Inventory and accountability

- Every AI system and agent is listed with its purpose and intended use, model and provider, accountable owner, the data it reaches and where that data is processed.
- The owner can stop the system at any time. The decisions that need a person, rather than the system, are declared in advance, and stopping and resuming are recorded.
- Changes to a system's model, instructions, tools, skills, data or schedule that need formal review are defined, each with an accountable lead, and go through the change management process.

## Risk and impact

- Before a system is deployed, and when it changes materially, its risks are assessed against the organization's AI risk taxonomy, together with the laws that apply to it and its impact on individuals, groups and society. The assessment and its treatment are recorded and reviewed at least yearly.

## Limits and safeguards

- Agents act only within the access, tools, spend and rate limits set for their task and role. Actions outside those limits are blocked, and one customer's data never reaches another customer's session.
- Safeguards keep AI output from disclosing confidential or personal data or secrets, infringing others' rights, giving harmful, out-of-scope or fabricated answers, producing insecure code, or assisting cyber or other serious misuse.

## Testing and monitoring

- Systems are tested before any release that needs approval, and at least quarterly by an independent tester, for adversarial robustness, harmful and out-of-scope output, hallucination and unsafe tool use. Findings are fixed or accepted by the owner, and recorded.
- The activity of AI systems and agents is recorded and retained under the logging and monitoring policy, and watched across the organization's risk categories.

## Data

- How customer data is used as AI input, how long it is kept, and who owns and may use AI output are stated to customers.
- Data used to build, tune, ground or evaluate a system is documented with its source, the right to use it, and the checks and preparation applied to it.

## Transparency and failures

- People are told when they are dealing with an AI system, what it is for and its limits, and how to give feedback or raise a concern.
- Plans for AI failures (a breach through an AI system, harmful output, a costly fabricated answer) name owners, notification and remediation. They are handled under the incident response policy.

## Acceptable use

Personnel use AI tools only as this policy and the acceptable use policy allow: no confidential or personal data in tools not approved for it, and no output used without the review its purpose requires.

The people who use {{organization}}'s AI systems are told what use is acceptable. Prohibited uses are defined for each system, detected where the system can detect them, and blocked, and the user is told when a use is blocked.

## Review

The policy owner reviews this policy at least yearly and after any significant AI incident, and records the review.
